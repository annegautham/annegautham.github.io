## Harry Frankfurt on the Freedom of the Will

Attempting to argue the compatibilist perspective.

A challenge for all positions:

- What is it to have free will?
  - Challenge for the incompatibilist position: how can there be choices and decisions in an deterministic world
  - If you decide/choose, you do so on the basis of reasons
    - If reasons determine choice, then deterministic
    - Chisholm solved this by
      - Doctrine of immanent causation
      - Desires and motives 'incline' but not 'necessitate'
  - Challenge for copatibilist position: how can one be said to decide between options if one's desicison were causally predetermined?
    - Hume states that
      - One acts freely when one acts as one desires
      - One's will is free when one would have done otherwise if one had desired otherwise
      - On this view, it doesn't matter if one's desire were themselves predetermined...

Frankfurt:

- He does not explain why determinism is true

Rather his view is a theory of free will, and points out that we satisfy his conditions on free will whether or not determinsim is satisfied. Frankfurt's account is one of a psychological picture:

- On frankfurts view, free will has to do with the structure of one's will
  - To say that the wil has a structure is to say that we can distinguish
    - One's desires
    - One's effective desires
    - One's desires about which of one's desires are effective

  Personhood is having a perspective on your psychology - things that provide you with potential reasons.
  Having a perspective on the internal war of desires that has a bias on which side wins (not a neutral observer).
  These second-order desire perspective is essential to personhood.

Free act vs. Free Will

- Free Will
  - One sees the chocolate cake and wants eat
    - But one also wants to make helathy choices when eating
    - This is a clash of first-order desires
    - Such individuals are wontons
  - Suppose what you really want is to act on your first-order desire to make a healthier choice, but if you fail in this regard, you give in to temptation and eat the cake
    - Then you are the unwilling addict (2nd-order desire is systematically overruled)
      - Addict is not free... but they do have free will...

The willing addict is such the second-order desire successfully implements the first-order desire...
